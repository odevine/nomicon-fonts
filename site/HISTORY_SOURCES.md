# History of the typeface: sources for review

Nothing here is on the site. The "Where the shapes come from" section of the Printing page is a plain note until you approve text and sources. This file lists what a first round of research found, with its sources and how firm each claim is.

## The typeface, fairly well documented

Monotype Plantin was drawn at the Monotype works in Salfords, England, in 1912 and 1913. Frank Hinman Pierpont led the work and the drawing office's Fritz Stelzer worked with him. It was released as Series 110 in 1913.

It follows, loosely, a Gros Cicero roman that Robert Granjon cut in 1569. Pierpont found it in a specimen of historic types published in 1905 by the Plantin-Moretus Museum in Antwerp.

The type in that specimen was worn, and was probably an eighteenth-century replacement cut. Some letters, including the `a` and the italic, are thought not to be Granjon's.

Monotype wanted a sturdier text face than many historic types, which looked thin on the smoother coated paper of the period.

Where the sources disagree:
- **Date.** One account puts Pierpont's visit to Antwerp in 1912 and another gives 1914 for the design. The release in 1913 is the most common date.
- **Which type.** Wikipedia and Klim both note that the Granjon type was used by the Plantin press only from the eighteenth century, so the name of the typeface does not mean the Antwerp printer used it in his own time.

These are secondary sources. Both rest on printed books, and I could not open the books. Monotype's own page for the family returned a 404. For a firm history the first thing to check is Morison's *A Tally of Types* and Mosley's writing directly.

## That Magic cards use it, not verified

Wikipedia's Plantin article says the body text of every Magic card is set in Plantin, with no citation. Fan sources, a forum post from 2005 and a print-shop guide, say rules text is set in "MPlantin", for the sets up to Magic 2014. I could not read the mtg.wiki pages (403) and I found no first-party statement from Wizards of the Coast.

What this project has is its own measurement. The scanned letters match the owner's copy of the font closely, with an overlap of 0.95 to 0.99 for each of the 16 glyphs, and the classifier only accepts a match above 0.85. That shows the cards' letters follow the design closely, and it is a finding of the project. It does not say what Wizards calls the font or whether they modified it.

Suggestion: say what we measured, and leave the claim about which font Magic uses out unless you can cite a Wizards source.

Monotype Plantin was drawn at Monotype's works in Salfords, England, by Frank Hinman Pierpont and Fritz Stelzer, and released as Series 110 in 1913. It follows, loosely, a Gros Cicero roman that Robert Granjon cut in 1569, which Pierpont found in a 1905 specimen from the Plantin-Moretus Museum in Antwerp. The type in that specimen was worn, probably an eighteenth-century replacement, and some of its letters are thought not to be Granjon's. Monotype wanted a sturdier face than many old types, which looked thin on the smoother paper of the time.
